# Visual rubric — frontend_design/agent_platform_site

Grade ONLY what the screenshots (375, 768, 1280, 1920 px, full page) and source files show. Each criterion
uses levels 1–5. Choose the highest level whose description is fully satisfied. Cite concrete evidence
(section, element, width). Do not reward length, number of sections beyond the brief, or effects for their
own sake. Requirement compliance (palette, components, accessibility) is graded automatically elsewhere —
do not re-grade it here except where it visibly harms the design.

## visual_quality — overall aesthetic quality and coherence
1. Unstyled or broken: browser defaults, overlapping/clipped elements, obviously unfinished.
2. Styled but amateurish: inconsistent styles between sections, crude colour blocks, poor alignment.
3. Competent template-level: consistent and tidy, but generic; little visual identity; some rough spots.
4. Professional: cohesive visual language (shapes, radii, iconography, imagery) across all sections and widths; no visible defects.
5. Exceptional: distinctive, memorable art direction executed precisely; would not look out of place on a top-tier enterprise SaaS site.

## hierarchy_composition — layout, hierarchy, rhythm, spacing
1. No discernible hierarchy; content is a wall of text or a random grid.
2. Headline is distinguishable but sections compete; spacing is uneven; line lengths too long at 1920 or cramped at 375.
3. Clear section structure and readable rhythm, with a few awkward breaks (orphaned cards, uneven gutters, weak mobile stacking).
4. Strong hierarchy (one focal point per section, clear CTA priority), consistent spacing scale, deliberate grid at every width.
5. Composition actively guides the eye through the story (problem → product → proof → price → action) at every width; mobile layouts are designed, not merely stacked.

## brand_fit — matches "calm, precise, trustworthy; technical but warm; enterprise"
1. Contradicts the brief (playful/cartoonish, crypto/hype aesthetics, aggressive effects).
2. Neutral/generic; could be any product.
3. Fits the brief in places (e.g. hero) but not throughout.
4. Consistently conveys a serious, trustworthy enterprise product; palette used with restraint (Mint as accent, Orbit for action).
5. As 4, plus original brand expression (illustration, product visualisation, typographic details) that makes the product concept tangible.

## polish — details and micro-interactions visible in the artefacts
1. Visible defects: misaligned elements, text overflowing containers, broken images, inconsistent button styles.
2. Several small defects (inconsistent radii, uneven card heights, cramped buttons, poor contrast in decorative text).
3. Mostly clean; one or two noticeable defects.
4. Clean at all widths: consistent states (hover/focus defined in CSS), aligned baselines, balanced cards, careful form styling.
5. Meticulous: consistent details down to icon stroke weights, numeric alignment in pricing, considered empty/success/error states.
